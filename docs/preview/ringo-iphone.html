Fog over the ridges, and the art of reading slowly

Slow Mornings
Lena Hartmann

Some mornings arrive in layers. First the water, then a band of fog, then one ridge after another, each a little paler than the last, until the sky takes over. Nothing moves quickly, and nothing asks to be noticed.

Reading can feel like that, too. A good article does not shout; it lets you settle in, one paragraph after the next, with enough space around the words that your eyes never have to fight for their place on the page.

Leave room for the text

Most of what makes a page calm is what is not there: no competing colors, no ornaments, no layout that changes its mind halfway down. A measured line length, generous leading and a quiet palette do most of the work.

A familiar view becomes new when you give it enough time.

What remains should be easy to find. Headings stand apart without raising their voice, links are clearly links, and the dateline and byline stay small enough to be read once and then forgotten.

Three things worth keeping

  • One typeface, set with care, in a few sizes.
  • Images that run wide when they have something to show.
  • Captions that whisper rather than repeat the paragraph above.

By the time the fog lifts, the ridges have flattened into an ordinary hillside again. The article ends the same way: quietly, with nothing left to dismiss.
